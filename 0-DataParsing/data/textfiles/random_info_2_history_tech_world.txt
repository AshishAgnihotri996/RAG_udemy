RANDOM INFORMATION FILE 2: HISTORY, TECH, LANGUAGE AND THE WORLD
=================================================================

1. Oxford University is older than the Aztec Empire. Teaching at Oxford existed by 1096, while the Aztec city of Tenochtitlan was founded in 1325.

2. Cleopatra lived closer in time to the Moon landing than to the construction of the Great Pyramid of Giza.

3. The Anglo-Zanzibar War of 1896 is often called the shortest war in history, lasting less than an hour.

4. The Eiffel Tower can grow taller by around 15 centimeters in summer because heat expands the metal.

5. The first email was sent by Ray Tomlinson in 1971, and he is credited with choosing the "@" symbol for addresses.

6. The first webcam was pointed at a coffee pot at the University of Cambridge so researchers could check if it was full.

7. Nintendo was founded in 1889 as a playing card company.

8. The word "robot" comes from the Czech word "robota," meaning forced labor, and was popularized by the 1920 play R.U.R.

9. Vatican City is the smallest country in the world by both area and population.

10. Scotland's national animal is the unicorn.

11. Canada has more lakes than any other country.

12. The Python programming language is named after the comedy group Monty Python, not the snake.

13. Bluetooth is named after Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson, a Danish king who united tribes. The logo combines his initials in ancient runes.

14. "Wi-Fi" does not officially stand for anything. It was a catchy brand name.

15. The concept of zero as a number was developed in ancient India.

16. "Strengths" is often cited as the longest English word with only one vowel.

17. The Great Fire of London in 1666 destroyed over 13,000 houses but officially recorded very few deaths.

18. The Library of Alexandria is one of the most famous libraries of the ancient world, though historians still debate how it declined.

19. The printing press, introduced to Europe by Johannes Gutenberg in the 15th century, helped spread knowledge faster than ever before.

20. Tokyo is the most populous metropolitan area in the world.

21. Fortune cookies were popularized in the United States, not China.

22. The first computer "bug" story comes from 1947, when a moth was found stuck in a Harvard Mark II relay.

23. The QWERTY keyboard layout was designed in the 1870s for typewriters.

24. Honeybees, ancient Egyptians, and modern chefs all agree on one thing: salt makes almost everything taste better.

25. The Great Wall of China is not one continuous wall but a series of walls and fortifications built over many centuries.
